01 · PRODUCTION PLATFORM · SOLE ENGINEER AND OWNER · JUL 2025 — NOV 2026

IronFist

Six neuroimaging pipelines, one platform, nobody babysitting the cluster.

Before. The lab ran FastSurfer, HippUnfold, micapipe, nnU-Net quality control and z-brains by hand, one subject at a time, and 55% of legacy sessions mixed software versions.

What I built. A Snakemake workflow in SHA-pinned Singularity containers on a 256-core SGE cluster, with a run registry, a lab-wide operations dashboard and a sign-in portal. Atomic symlink releases with health checks and one-command rollback. A lock file that pins nine components and two model weights and is verified on every run. 1,394 tests and GitHub Actions CI.

What changed. A 692-session campaign was projected at three days and failed silently at scale. I profiled SGE slot reservation, re-tuned threads and added automatic resubmission: 34 hours at twice the throughput, zero failures, five scale-only faults fixed. The platform now tracks 1,100+ sessions across four cohorts and was handed over with a runbook, an eight-page wiki and a meeting with the two PIs.

02 · DATA PIPELINE · SOLE ENGINEER · 2025 — 2026

AutoDICOM and the BIDS rebuild

New scans arrive in the research dataset within the hour, and nothing ever gets overwritten.

Before. Scans were sorted and converted by hand with shell scripts, days after acquisition, and the lab’s 1.4 TB dataset failed the BIDS validator 24,915 times.

What I built. An hourly service that finds new sessions, waits until files have been stable for four hours, converts DICOM to NIfTI and publishes each BIDS session atomically. It never overwrites an existing session and never publishes a partial one. A main → prod promotion gate in CI, a read-only dashboard, 200 tests. For the legacy data, a non-destructive migration that recorded all 23,767 actions and left the source untouched.

What changed. In production since August 2026: 718 sessions from 518 subjects converted, none needing manual attention. Validator errors: zero.

  • 718 sessions
  • 0 needing attention
  • 24,915 → 0 validator errors
  • 200 tests

03 · CLOUD AND PRODUCT · INFRA AND CI/CD OWNER ON A 3-PERSON TEAM · 2026

brainscores.ai

A researcher picks a scan; forty minutes later there is a PDF report and a 3D viewer.

Before. The startup needed a public site with sign-in and study uploads on Canadian-resident infrastructure, and researchers had to ask an engineer to run the MICAFlow pipeline.

What I built. The infrastructure and delivery for the Next.js site (38 of 39 infra commits): Terraform for ECS Fargate on ARM64, CloudFront and ALB, Cognito sign-in, presigned uploads straight to S3 with server-side size limits, DynamoDB. GitHub Actions deploys with OIDC and no stored keys, main → dev → prod promotion, Terraform contract tests. Then the self-serve pipeline: a FastAPI service and portal that runs MICAFlow on 24 SGE cores and hands the result to the viewer through a ten-minute HMAC-signed link, with every component version pinned and verified per run (538 tests). On the clinical viewer itself, built by a teammate, I added Playwright end-to-end tests, render-safety and left/right checks and a deploy gate that blocks releases without verification evidence.

What changed. Live since July 2026; the dev environment costs about $25 a month.

04 · OPEN SOURCE · LEAD MAINTAINER · 2024 — 2026

BrainSpace, BrainStat, micapipe

Three libraries thousands of researchers depend on, back on a release cadence.

Before. BrainSpace and BrainStat, cited by 800+ papers, had not had a release in two to three years. Issues piled up, CI was red, and the lab’s flagship pipeline micapipe was a 2,000-line bash workflow.

What I did. Took over as lead maintainer: triaged and closed 80+ issues, reviewed external pull requests, rebuilt CI for Python 3.10–3.12 with tag-validated publishing to PyPI and MATLAB, added a Docker image and contribution and security policies, and shipped 14 releases including a WebGL surface report and new atlases. Led the micapipe v1 rewrite with its original author (495 of about 500 v1 commits): Snakemake/snakebids, eight modules, two-stage Docker and Apptainer builds so the 50 GB tool image rebuilds in minutes, lint and smoke-test CI on a self-hosted runner. Replaced two C++ dependencies with Python ports validated against the originals: zb_command (27 Connectome Workbench commands, 22 bit-exact) and pyonlm (correlation 1.0000 with mincnlm, 1.4× faster).

What changed. Zero open issues, about 6,800 downloads a month, and a pipeline other labs can actually run.

05 · DEEP LEARNING FOR MRI · RESEARCH ENGINEER · 2025 — 2026

Models that run on a real cluster

Training 3D networks, finding the bug that froze one, and picking the serving path.

Segmentation. The lab needed a license-clean replacement for SynthSeg. I trained a 66M-parameter residual 3D U-Net with SynthSeg-style domain randomization over 13 tissue and pathology classes, with PyTorch DDP on four A6000 GPUs, then fine-tuned on 93 real subjects. On a held-out real T1 it reaches white- and grey-matter Dice of 0.83 and 0.89 against 0.89 and 0.92 for SynthSeg itself, at 16 seconds a volume.

Surfaces. A Neural-ODE mesh-deformation model for cortical surfaces was stuck at a loss plateau. I traced it to an adjoint bug that silently froze the U-Net encoder, fixed a gradient bias in the solver, and added Huber loss, gradient checkpointing and mixed precision.

Serving. Benchmarked ONNX Runtime and OpenVINO for the lab’s two inference models: 2.4× faster on CPU, 2.1× on GPU, bit-identical outputs. The team adopted the audit.

  • 66M parameters
  • 13 classes
  • 4× A6000, DDP
  • 2.4× faster on CPU
